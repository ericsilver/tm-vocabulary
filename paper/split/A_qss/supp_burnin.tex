\subsection{Thin references near the start of the record}\label{sec:burnin}

\emph{Burn-in} is the span at each end of the corpus where the reference
windows cannot be filled, so scores there reflect a thin reference rather
than the text being scored.

A filing's score depends on having enough same-class filings on each
side of it. A full five-year window exists on both sides for filings
made 1990 through 2020, and the production scoring runs 1995--2019,
holding back five years at the start of that span, where references are
thinnest, and one at the end; a filing is also scored only when
each of its windows holds at least 500 filings. Figure~\ref{fig:burnin}
shows how reference density builds up at the start of the record. How
far a thin reference distorts the score was profiled on an earlier build
of the measure (word-scored, with calendar-year references): a thin past
reference inflates past-facing surprise, and across the classes with
enough volume to profile, the median distortion was about 2 nats for
filings made in 1990 and fell quickly as the window filled. On that
build's own criterion, though, 18 of 44 classes stayed noisy past 1995,
into 1996--2004, so estimates that lean
on the earliest cohorts --- the companion paper's era comparisons, in
particular --- are read with that in mind.
