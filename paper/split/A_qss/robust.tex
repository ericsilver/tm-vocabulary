\section{Robustness: what changes under other choices}\label{sec:robust}

Each result above is stated under one formulation chosen for legibility:
fifty global themes, flat weighting inside the reference windows,
symmetric five-year windows, estimates conditional on grant, and the
2002--2018 cohorts. Table~\ref{tab:robust} summarizes what each choice
costs; the supplementary material (\S S4) carries the full treatment,
each variation run on the same filings wherever the variation allows.

Two findings from the sweep bear on any reuse of the measure. First,
because a single fit's lead score carries measurement error of roughly a
quarter of its variance at fifty themes (a fifth at 200), every
coefficient on lead is attenuated toward zero; correcting for it would
enlarge the estimates, not shrink them. Averaging scores over several
refits is a cheap way for users of the measure to reduce that noise.
Second, lead is a small residual of two large, highly correlated levels
($r = 0.99$), which is what makes it sensitive to anything that
inflates the levels --- under term scoring, document length --- and is
why the theme representation, where the level spread is stable, is the
one on which a unit of lead means the same thing everywhere.
