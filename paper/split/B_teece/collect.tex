\section{Who collects within a wave of entry}\label{sec:collect}

\emph{Pioneers and early followers fail alike.} Within each class, a
curated vocabulary arrives in the first year at least five of the class's
filings use it. Registrations using a new vocabulary in its first two
years in the class are out of use at the five-year declaration 12.8
points more often than other registrations of the same class and year;
those two to five years in, 11.8 points more often; those six or more
years in, 6.0. The first two groups do not differ ($t = 0.5$). The
pioneers of these markets fail, as \citet{GolderTellis1993} found in
named histories, but so do the firms that follow them in the next few
years; the cost belongs to joining the wave early, not to being first in
it.

\emph{Buyers do not favour early entrants.} A business can also be
rewarded by being bought. Recorded transfers of trademark ownership
between companies show that 0.31\% of first-time owners had their first
mark bought by another company, almost twice the share that listed. The
most leading fifth of first registrations were bought slightly less often
than the most lagging (0.29\% against 0.33\%, $t = -2.1$), and bought by
an established firm, one holding 25 or more marks, two-thirds as often
(0.046\% against 0.069\%, $t = -3.1$).
