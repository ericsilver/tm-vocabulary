\section{Filing, drafting, and reaching registration}\label{sec:registration}

This section is about the first step only: whether an application reaches
registration, on every class-record filed 1995--2018. The companion paper
treats this step as a validation of the measure \citep{SilverCorpus2026};
what matters here is the contrast it sets up with the steps that follow.

\subsection{Registration rises with atypicality and barely moves with lead}\label{sec:reg_rates}

Figure~\ref{fig:regdeciles} gives the registration rate by decile of each
axis, with deciles cut within Nice class and filing year so that no
difference between industries or between years enters. On atypicality the
rate rises from 56.0\% in the least atypical decile to 59.1\% in the ninth
and turns down to 58.3\% in the most atypical --- a three-point climb and
a one-point turn at the top. On lead the range is 0.9 points: filings
written in language their class was moving toward register at nearly the
same rate as filings written in language it was leaving.

The flatness of lead at this step has a mechanical reading worth stating:
lead is computed from the five years of filings \emph{after} the filing
date, which had not happened when the application was examined. No
participant in prosecution could observe the quantity. Atypicality's
past-facing half, by contrast, is exactly what an examiner steeped in the
class's usual language experiences, and it is the axis registration
responds to. The shape --- rising, then turning at the extreme --- is the
one audience-conformity research predicts for evaluators
\citep{Zuckerman1999, Hsu2006, Zhao2017}, with the caution that
registration is mostly a measure of the applicant's own follow-through:
of the applications that do not register, roughly nine in ten die by
inaction, and only 4.3\% are removed adversarially
(Table~\ref{tab:funnel}).

\subsection{Drafting and refiling}\label{sec:representation}\label{sec:amend}

Who drafts the description matters for everything that follows.
Represented applications are 73\% of filings over 1995--2018 and reach
registration at 61.9\%, against 45.6\% for self-filed ones; self-filers
write shorter descriptions, sit closer to their class's usual language,
and lean slightly further ahead of it. Because who drafts moves both the
language and the outcome at once, counsel of record is held in every
five-year-proof regression, and Section~\ref{sec:gate} reports the
estimates separately by drafter.

Applicants who try again learn to conform. Among 2.28 million abandoned
applications, 3.6\% are followed within six years by the same owner
filing the same mark in the same class, and 1.5\% by one that then
registers. Of the 42{,}115 abandoned applications whose owner later
registered the same mark in the same class, 74\% carry a rewritten
description, and the rewrite moves toward the class's typical language
from both tails: originals in the most atypical fifth lose $0.39$ nats of
atypicality, and originals in the most conventional fifth gain $0.16$,
whoever drafts the second attempt. Refilings that reuse the identical
text move by less than $0.01$, so the convergence is in the writing (the
companion paper gives the detail by drafter).

Three facts carry forward. Registration is a follow-through indicator,
not an examiner's verdict: nine in ten failures are the applicant's own
inaction. Registration peaks at a middling distance from the class's
usual language and barely moves with lead, which no one at this stage
could observe. And applicants who try again conform --- while an
applicant who conformed before ever filing is unobserved, so that part
of the compression cannot be bounded. Every estimate on registered marks
in the sections that follow is an estimate on this partly conformed
language.
