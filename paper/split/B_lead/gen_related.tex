\section{Pioneering, conformity, and the trademark record}\label{sec:related}

The pioneering literature has measured first-mover advantage mostly on
named markets and survivor-prone samples \citep{GolderTellis1993,
SemadeniAnderson2010}. A parallel literature finds evaluators reward
conformity with a penalty for extremity in both directions --- the
optimal distinctiveness of \citet{Zuckerman1999}, \citet{Hsu2006},
\citet{Zhao2017}, and \citet{TaeuscherRothe2020}. This paper meets both
at census scale: every entrant's language is observed, whether or not
the product survived to be remembered, and the record dates two steps
that respond to the two components differently. Registration peaks at a
middling distance from the class's usual language and cannot observe
lead; the five-year proof of continued use penalizes having been early.

The data and the measure come from a companion paper
\citep{SilverCorpus2026}, which places them in the literature that reads
trademarks as innovation indicators \citep{Castaldi2020, Flikkema2019,
Dinlersoz2018} and in the text-as-data work that scores documents
against what came before and after them \citep{Murdock2017,
Barron2018}, and which explains the names used here: \emph{lead} rather
than resonance, which asserts a mechanism, and \emph{atypicality}
rather than novelty or radicalness, which claim more than a
description's position can show \citep{Verhoeven2025}.
