\section{The measure and the record in brief}\label{sec:measure}\label{sec:construct}

\subsection{Two readers, one living backwards}

Imagine two readers of a filing's goods/services description, each having
seen a disjoint half of its industry. One has read only what the industry
filed in the five years before it. The other has read only the five years
after. Each is asked how surprising the wording is. The measurement is how
much they disagree: a description that startles the first reader and not
the second arrived before its industry's language moved that way.

Formally, each description is reduced to a bag of words and re-described
in a fixed set of fifty themes by latent Dirichlet allocation, fitted once
over a stratified sample of all 45 Nice classes; a theme is a cluster of
words that co-occur across filings (\emph{video, music, games,
electronic, audio}), a component many offerings share rather than a
subject. Write $P$ for a filing's distribution over themes, and $Q^{-}$
and $Q^{+}$ for that distribution averaged over every same-class filing
in the 1{,}826 days before and after its filing date. The filing's
past-facing and future-facing surprise are the Kullback--Leibler
divergences $K^{-} = \mathrm{KL}(P \,\|\, Q^{-})$ and
$K^{+} = \mathrm{KL}(P \,\|\, Q^{+})$, in nats. The two axes used
throughout are their average and their signed difference:
\[
  A \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\left(K^{-} + K^{+}\right) \quad\text{(atypicality)},
  \qquad
  L \;=\; K^{-} - K^{+} \quad\text{(lead)} .
\]
A filing is \emph{leading} when $L > 0$: its language was closer to what
the class wrote afterwards than to what it wrote before. The levels
correlate at $0.988$, so the rotation isolates a small residual that
carries the timing from a large common component that carries the
distance, and leaves the two regressors nearly uncorrelated. \textsc{Amazon.com}'s
1997 filing in advertising and retail sits at the 47th percentile of its
class on atypicality and the 94th on lead: the class moved toward its
description over the following five years and had not been writing that
way in the five before.

Three properties of the construction matter for reading the results.
First, themes rather than words: a divergence measured on words mostly
counts description length, because the spread term of the divergence is
bounded by the filing's distinct-term count, and term-scored atypicality
correlates with log distinct terms at $-0.68$; theme scoring breaks the
coupling ($-0.22$). Second, within class rather than across it:
goods/services descriptions are drafted from the USPTO's Identification
Manual, house precedent and competitors' filings, so 17\% of scored
registrations share a value with another filing in the same class and
year, and a class's spread of atypicality reflects its drafting
conventions rather than its inventiveness. Every estimate is taken within
class and year, and magnitudes compare across classes only as signs and
orderings. Third, the measure reads which themes a filing draws on and
how heavily, not how it combines them; combinatorial novelty is close to
invisible to it, and the companion paper bounds that omission.

The companion paper rescores the identical filings under three partitions
of the vocabulary (fifty global themes, five hundred, fifty fitted per
class), two reference weightings, five window constructions, and a second
model seed. Lead's sign, precision and profile survive all of them, and
term scoring returns larger lead contrasts than the theme scoring used
here, so the estimates below are the conservative representation.
Atypicality is less stable, in ways Section~\ref{sec:three_scorings}
reports where they bear on the results. Nomenclature follows
\citet{Murdock2017} and \citet{Barron2018} in arithmetic and departs from
them in names; Table~\ref{tab:nomenclature} maps the terms.
