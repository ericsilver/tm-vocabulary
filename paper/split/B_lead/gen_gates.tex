\subsection{The record's steps, the samples, and the conventions}\label{sec:gates}

A US trademark application is filed with a description of the goods or
services; an examiner reviews it, often raising objections the applicant
must answer; and if the application is allowed --- and, for marks filed
on an intent to use, once the applicant swears the mark is in use --- it
registers. Between the fifth and sixth year after registration the owner
must file a sworn declaration that the mark is still in use in commerce,
the \emph{five-year proof of continued use}, and around year ten a
renewal. A mark whose owner misses a deadline is cancelled. The companion
paper builds the event-dated record that separates the two deadlines and
tabulates the full funnel \citep{SilverCorpus2026}; the rates this paper
leans on are these. Of 3.59 million owners' first filings made
1995--2018, 57.6\% registered; of the applications that did not, roughly
nine in ten ended by the applicant's own inaction and 4.3\% by an
adversarial removal. Of 3.10 million registrations issued 2002--2018,
52.3\% were cancelled at the five-year proof --- which means the product
the mark named had left commerce within six years --- and of those that
passed it, 39.1\% were cancelled at the year-ten renewal.

Outcomes are rates, so every estimate is a shift in a probability, stated
in percentage points against the base rate it shifts: $+1.4$pp on a
52.3\% base means 53.7\% against 52.3\%. The basic comparison is the
outcome rate of the fifth of filings with the highest lead minus that of
the fifth with the lowest, with the fifths cut inside a single Nice class
and year so that no difference between industries or between years
enters. A \emph{registration} counts a filing once by serial number; a
\emph{debut filing} is an owner's first, one per owner; a \emph{cohort}
is the registrations issued in a calendar year. Scoring covers filings
made 1995--2019, and five-year-proof cohorts run 2002--2018, the last
cohort whose proof window has essentially elapsed in a record that ends
in April 2026. Carried back to the first scoreable cohorts, the penalty
was larger than it has ever been since (Section~\ref{sec:eras}).
